Research

My work centers on precise astrometry and photometry of small Solar System bodies in dense stellar fields, and on developing the next generation of measurement tools — including machine learning and AI.

Master’s research (Observatório Nacional)

Astrometria e fotometria de pequenos corpos do Sistema Solar em imagens com campos estelares densos · Advisor: Dr. Julio I. Bueno de Camargo · Co-advisor: Dr. Adriano Pieres · Dissertation PDF

Astrometry and photometry of moving objects in crowded fields are limited by flux contamination from neighboring stars. The dissertation evaluates and applies difference image analysis (DIA) to reduce that contamination and support routine dense-field observations for stellar-occultation predictions and physical characterization.

Methods and results

  • Simulated dense field (globular-cluster-like): compared PRAIA and DAOPHOT for reference-star centroids where DIA does not apply; PRAIA showed competitive performance under high stellar density.
  • DIA with DIAPL2 on a SOAR/SOI sequence of the centaur (10199) Chariklo in a dense field: build a model image without the moving target, subtract from re-sampled science frames, measure Chariklo on the difference images and reference stars with PRAIA on the re-sampled frames.
  • Astrometry: residual scatter versus the NIMA ephemeris improved substantially (e.g. from 0.066″ to 0.018″ in ∆α cos δ).
  • Photometry: difference-image fluxes yielded Chariklo’s rotational light curve and rotational phase with σφ = 0.0025.

(10199) Chariklo as science case

Chariklo is a benchmark centaur (rings revealed by stellar occultations). SOAR/SOI imaging in a dense field provides a demanding test of whether DIA recovers cleaner positions and a usable light curve when classical photometry on the science frames is contaminated.

Doctoral research direction — astrometric code with ML & AI

I am beginning a doctorate (doutorado) in the same research line, with a primary focus on developing astrometric software that uses machine learning and artificial intelligence. Building on the MSc results, intended themes include:

  • Learning-based or hybrid methods for source detection and centroiding under crowding and complex PSFs;
  • Robust measurement of moving objects when classical DIA or PSF fitting is limited;
  • Reproducible pipelines connecting classical tools (PRAIA-class workflows, DIA) to modern ML tooling;
  • Applications to centaurs, TNOs, and other small bodies for occultations and survey follow-up.
