Research

My research addresses high-precision astrometry and photometry of small Solar System bodies in dense stellar fields, and the development of measurement software—including methods based on machine learning and artificial intelligence.

Master’s thesis — Observatório Nacional

Astrometria e fotometria de pequenos corpos do Sistema Solar em imagens com campos estelares densos
Supervisor: Dr. Julio I. Bueno de Camargo · Co-supervisor: Dr. Adriano Pieres · PDF

In crowded fields, flux from neighboring stars contaminates measurements of moving targets and degrades both positions and light curves. The thesis evaluates and applies difference image analysis (DIA) to mitigate that contamination and to support dense-field programs aimed at stellar occultation prediction and physical characterization.

Approach and principal results

  • Controlled tests on a simulated dense field (globular-cluster-like): comparison of PRAIA and DAOPHOT for reference-star centroids in regimes where DIA is not applicable; PRAIA remained competitive at high stellar density.
  • DIA with DIAPL2 on a SOAR/SOI time series of the centaur (10199) Chariklo: construction of a static model frame, subtraction from re-sampled science images, measurement of the moving source on the residuals, and measurement of reference stars with PRAIA on the re-sampled frames.
  • Astrometry: residual scatter relative to the NIMA ephemeris decreased substantially (e.g., from 0.066″ to 0.018″ in ∆α cos δ).
  • Photometry: fluxes on difference images yielded a rotational light curve and a rotational phase with σφ = 0.0025.

Science case: (10199) Chariklo

Chariklo is a well-studied centaur whose ring system was revealed by stellar occultations. SOAR/SOI imaging against a dense stellar background provides a stringent test of whether DIA recovers cleaner astrometry and a scientifically usable light curve when aperture or PSF photometry on the original frames is compromised by crowding.

Doctoral research directions

I am beginning doctoral studies in the same area, with emphasis on astrometric software that incorporates machine learning and artificial intelligence. Building on the master’s results, near-term themes include:

  • Learning-based or hybrid approaches to detection and centroiding under crowding and complex PSFs;
  • Robust measurement of moving objects when classical DIA or PSF fitting is limited;
  • Reproducible pipelines that connect established tools (PRAIA-class reductions, DIA) to modern ML frameworks;
  • Applications to centaurs, trans-Neptunian objects, and related populations for occultation work and survey follow-up.
