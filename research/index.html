Research

My work centers on precise measurements of small Solar System bodies in crowded stellar fields, and on developing the next generation of astrometric tools — including machine learning and AI — for survey-scale and targeted imaging.

Master’s research (Observatório Nacional)

Astrometria e fotometria de pequenos corpos do Sistema Solar em imagens com campos estelares densos · Advisor: Dr. Julio I. Bueno de Camargo · Co-advisor: Dr. Adriano Pieres · Dissertation PDF

Wide surveys such as the Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST) will deliver multiband photometry and astrometry for millions of Solar System objects. For stellar occultations, however, sky regions and regimes not fully covered by such surveys still demand dedicated, high-quality measurements — especially where stellar density is high and the target flux is contaminated by neighbors.

The dissertation applies and evaluates the image-difference technique (DIAPL2) to isolate the flux of a small body in dense fields, aiming at position and flux measurements with minimal influence from nearby sources, with the intention of routine use in observing programs. Methodology and tests include:

  • Simulated dense-field images to characterize astrometric and photometric performance;
  • Comparisons involving PRAIA and IRAF/DAOPHOT;
  • Difference imaging (DIAPL2) on real data of the centaur (10199) Chariklo;
  • Assessment of residual systematics relevant to orbit quality and occultation prediction.

(10199) Chariklo as science case

Chariklo is a benchmark centaur with a ring system revealed by stellar occultations. It provides a demanding test of astrometry and photometry when the object moves across dense stellar backgrounds. Difference imaging was used to suppress the static field and recover cleaner measurements of the moving source.

Doctoral research direction — astrometric code with ML & AI

I am beginning a doctorate (doutorado) in the same research line, with a primary focus on developing astrometric software that uses machine learning and artificial intelligence. Building on the MSc results, intended themes include:

  • Learning-based or hybrid methods for source detection and centroiding under crowding and complex PSFs;
  • Robust measurement of moving objects when classical difference imaging or PSF fitting is limited;
  • Reproducible, scalable pipelines connecting classical astrometry (e.g. PRAIA-class workflows) to modern ML tooling;
  • Applications to centaurs, TNOs, and other small-body populations for occultations and survey follow-up.
