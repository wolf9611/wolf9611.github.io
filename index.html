I work on high-precision astrometry and photometry of small Solar System bodies, with emphasis on measurements in dense stellar fields.

I earned a Licenciatura in Physics (undergraduate degree) from the Universidade Estadual Vale do Acaraú (UVA, Ceará, Brazil) and an M.S. in Astronomy from the Observatório Nacional (ON, Rio de Janeiro), under the supervision of Dr. Julio I. Bueno de Camargo and the co-supervision of Dr. Adriano Pieres. My master’s thesis, Astrometria e fotometria de pequenos corpos do Sistema Solar em imagens com campos estelares densos (PDF), develops and tests difference image analysis (DIA) with DIAPL2 to isolate the flux of moving targets in crowded fields. After benchmarking PRAIA against DAOPHOT on a simulated dense field for reference-star centroids, I applied DIA to a SOAR/SOI time series of the centaur (10199) Chariklo. Combining difference-image positions with PRAIA reference stars reduced the residual scatter relative to the NIMA ephemeris (e.g., from 0.066″ to 0.018″ in ∆α cos δ); difference-image photometry recovered a rotational light curve with phase uncertainty σφ = 0.0025. The broader goal is to establish DIA as a routine tool for dense-field programs that support stellar occultation predictions and physical characterization.

I am beginning doctoral studies along the same research trajectory—small-body astrometry and photometry—with a focus on astrometric software development that integrates machine learning and artificial intelligence, targeting robust measurements under crowding and scalable pipelines for wide-field survey data.
