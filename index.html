Hi — welcome. I am Josué da Silva Cavalcante, an astronomer working on the astrometry and photometry of small bodies in the Solar System.

I hold a Bachelor’s degree in Physics from the Universidade Estadual Vale do Acaraú (UVA, Ceará, Brazil). I completed my Master’s degree in Astronomy at the Observatório Nacional (ON, Rio de Janeiro). My MSc research — dissertation Astrometria e fotometria de pequenos corpos do Sistema Solar em imagens com campos estelares densos — focused on precise positional and photometric measurements of small bodies in dense stellar fields, including work connected to the centaur (10199) Chariklo, using tools and pipelines from the Brazilian astrometric ecosystem (e.g. PRAIA / DIA-related workflows).

Alongside research, I teach Ciências da Natureza in secondary education in Ceará, and I care about clear scientific communication between survey-scale Solar System science and the classroom.

I am starting my PhD in the same research line — small Solar System bodies, astrometry and photometry — with a focus on developing astrometric software that incorporates machine learning and artificial intelligence, aiming at more robust measurements in crowded fields and at scalable pipelines for wide-field survey data.

An overview of research themes is on the Research page. A short CV is here; thesis and publications are under Publications. For Portuguese, see Português.
