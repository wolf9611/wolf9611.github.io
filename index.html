Hi — welcome. I am Josué da Silva Cavalcante, an astronomer working on the astrometry and photometry of small bodies in the Solar System.

I hold a Bachelor’s degree in Physics from the Universidade Estadual Vale do Acaraú (UVA, Ceará, Brazil). I completed my Master’s degree in Astronomy at the Observatório Nacional (ON, Rio de Janeiro), advised by Dr. Julio I. Bueno de Camargo and co-advised by Dr. Adriano Pieres. My dissertation, Astrometria e fotometria de pequenos corpos do Sistema Solar em imagens com campos estelares densos (PDF), applies and evaluates image-difference techniques (DIAPL2) to isolate the flux of a moving small body in dense stellar fields, reducing contamination from nearby stars and improving astrometric positions and photometry. The work combines simulated images and real observations of the centaur (10199) Chariklo, using tools such as PRAIA, IRAF/DAOPHOT and difference imaging, in the context of precise measurements for stellar-occultation predictions — especially in sky regions and crowded fields that wide surveys like LSST will not fully replace.

I am starting my PhD in the same research line — small Solar System bodies, astrometry and photometry — with a focus on developing astrometric software that incorporates machine learning and artificial intelligence, aiming at more robust measurements in crowded fields and at scalable pipelines for wide-field survey data.
