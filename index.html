Hi — welcome. I am Josué da Silva Cavalcante, an astronomer working on the astrometry and photometry of small bodies in the Solar System.

I hold a Bachelor’s degree in Physics from the Universidade Estadual Vale do Acaraú (UVA, Ceará, Brazil). I completed my Master’s degree in Astronomy at the Observatório Nacional (ON, Rio de Janeiro), advised by Dr. Julio I. Bueno de Camargo and co-advised by Dr. Adriano Pieres. My dissertation, Astrometria e fotometria de pequenos corpos do Sistema Solar em imagens com campos estelares densos (PDF), evaluates difference image analysis (DIA) with DIAPL2 to isolate the flux of moving small bodies in dense stellar fields. After validating PRAIA against DAOPHOT on a simulated crowded field for reference-star centroids, DIA was applied to a SOAR/SOI sequence of the centaur (10199) Chariklo: positions from difference images plus PRAIA reference stars improved the scatter relative to the NIMA ephemeris (e.g. from 0.066″ to 0.018″ in ∆α cos δ), and difference-image photometry yielded a rotational light curve with precise phase (σφ = 0.0025). The goal is to make DIA routine for dense-field observations supporting stellar-occultation predictions and physical characterization.

I am beginning my doctorate (doutorado) in the same research line — small Solar System bodies, astrometry and photometry — with a focus on developing astrometric software that incorporates machine learning and artificial intelligence, aiming at more robust measurements in crowded fields and at scalable pipelines for wide-field survey data.
